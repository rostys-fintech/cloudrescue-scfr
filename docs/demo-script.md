# 3–5 minute judge demo script — CloudRescue

## Target length
**3:30–4:15**

The goal is not to show every feature. The goal is to prove four things quickly:

1. the systemic-risk problem is real;
2. the mechanism is understandable;
3. the prototype is genuinely interactive and reproducible;
4. the project demonstrates learning and technical growth.

---

## 0:00–0:20 — Hook

**On screen:** Landing page.

Say:

> A backup plan can work for one bank and still fail for the system if many banks need the same backup capacity at once. CloudRescue is a synthetic stress lab for that coordination problem.

Then:

> The real-world motivation is third-party ICT concentration risk. The numbers in this prototype are deliberately synthetic.

Click **Run guided replay**.

---

## 0:20–1:15 — Let the guided story do the explaining

Do **not** talk over every narration line.

Let the built-in narrator and visuals show:

1. shared provider dependency;
2. provider outage;
3. simultaneous emergency-capacity demand;
4. shortage;
5. ring-fenced reserve that cannot move;
6. pooled SCFR reserve;
7. final comparison.

At Scene 4, briefly add:

> Watch the reserve tokens. Capacity exists, but ring-fencing keeps some of it in the wrong places.

At Scene 5:

> The important constraint is that SCFR does not receive a bigger reserve budget.

At Scene 6:

> Same shock. Same reserve. Different coordination.

Exit Focus View.

---

## 1:15–2:15 — Stress Lab: prove that it is not a hard-coded animation

Open **Stress Lab**.

Show the baseline results for only a few seconds.

Say:

> The story is generated by the same simulation engine as the interactive Stress Lab.

Change:

- provider outage;
- emergency-market capacity;
- reserve capacity.

Point to **Scenario ID**.

Say:

> Every set of assumptions gets a reproducible Scenario ID.

Click **Replay scenario**.

Let one or two scenes run, then stop.

Say:

> The guided story has now rebuilt itself around the scenario I just created.

Return to **Stress Lab**.

---

## 2:15–2:45 — Show the central experimental result

Scroll to **Controlled Comparison**.

Say:

> This is the cleanest comparison in the project. Both sides use exactly the same pre-reserved capacity. Individual reserves keep it ring-fenced; SCFR makes the same pool movable across affected banks.

Point to:

- identical reserve budget;
- stranded reserve;
- affected-bank recovery;
- resilience uplift.

Do not spend time reading every metric.

---

## 2:45–3:10 — Sensitivity, not one cherry-picked result

Open **Advanced analysis**, then show **Sensitivity Explorer**.

Say:

> One scenario is not enough to understand a mechanism, so I recalculate the model over a grid of market-capacity and reserve assumptions.

Open **Advanced analysis** and switch once:

**SCFR score → Coordination uplift**

Say:

> This view shows where coordination matters most and where it adds less value.

---

## 3:10–3:30 — Reproducibility and challenge mode

Scroll back to the left controls.

Enter a seed such as:

**FIRSTCOMMIT**

Click **Generate**.

Say:

> The seed deterministically creates the same scenario every time.

Click **Copy scenario link**.

Then show **Constraint Tests** briefly.

Say:

> Constraint Tests turn the stress lab into an experiment: can I achieve resilience under explicit resource constraints?

No need to solve every challenge in the video.

---

## 3:30–3:55 — Technical and research transparency

Open **Methodology**.

Show **How it works**.

Say:

> Under the interface is a deterministic allocation model with explicit assumptions, automated model invariants and interface checks.

Scroll to **Real-world motivation**.

Say:

> I also separate what is observed from what is synthetic. ICT concentration risk is real and documented. The bank network and all numerical outputs in v0.1 are synthetic.

---

## 3:55–4:15 — Learning & Growth close

Show **Development Journey · FirstCommit**.

Say:

> My background is finance and banking, not software engineering. I started with a systemic-risk question and learned how to turn it into explicit computational assumptions, a simulation engine, dynamic visualizations, reproducible scenarios, automated testing and a deployed product.

Final line:

> CloudRescue makes one idea visible: a plan B for one bank may not be a plan B for the system when everyone needs it at once.

End on either:

- the CloudRescue landing page, or
- the Controlled Comparison screen.

---

# Recording guidance

Use the **default light application theme** unless the full dark theme looks clearly better on the recording machine. The hero, live-system canvas, control rail and Controlled Comparison are already dark by design.

Before recording:

- CloudRescue automatically selects the preferred available English narrator;
- set browser zoom to 90–100%;
- close bookmarks / unnecessary browser panels;
- use Focus View for the guided story;
- keep cursor movement deliberate;
- never scroll rapidly through long sections;
- pause for 1–2 seconds after every important visual change;
- record at 1080p if possible.

Do not narrate every metric. Let the interface carry the detail.

The three phrases worth repeating verbally are:

> **Same shock. Same reserve budget. Different coordination.**

> **The real-world problem is observed; the numerical model is synthetic.**

> **This is a mechanism stress-test, not a forecast.**
