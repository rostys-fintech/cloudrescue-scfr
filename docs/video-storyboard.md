# Resilience Atlas — final LovHack demo storyboard

Target length: **2:50–2:59**

Goal: show the working product, not a presentation about the product.

## Recording setup

- Desktop browser, 16:9.
- 1920×1080 preferred.
- Browser zoom: 90–100%.
- Start on Simulation.
- Close unrelated tabs.
- Keep cursor movement deliberate.
- Do not speak over the built-in narrator.
- Pause briefly on the strongest screens.

## 0:00–0:19 — Research origin overlay

### Screen
The live Resilience Atlas interface remains visible behind a compact research overlay.

The overlay shows:

**Research → Shared cloud risk → SCFR → Resilience Atlas**

### Narration

> Resilience Atlas grew out of my broader research on what affects bank resilience and the role of digitalisation in it. I narrowed that question to one systemic cloud-resilience problem: when many banks share critical cloud infrastructure, can better coordination improve recovery without increasing the total reserve budget?

The overlay fades away. Press **Run Guided Simulation**.

## 0:14–0:58 — Guided Simulation

Let the built-in narration carry the mechanism.

Show:
1. shared dependency;
2. provider failure;
3. simultaneous recovery demand;
4. stranded ring-fenced reserve;
5. pooled SCFR reserve;
6. outcome comparison.

Only add short commentary in gaps:

> Capacity can exist and still be stranded in the wrong place.

Then:

> SCFR gets the same aggregate reserve budget. The difference is coordination.

At the comparison:

> Same shock. Same reserve budget. Different coordination.

## 0:58–1:35 — Scenario Lab

Open **Scenario Lab**.

Say:

> This is not a prerecorded animation. The same deterministic engine drives the interactive lab.

Change:
- failed provider;
- one capacity assumption.

Press **Run Scenario**.

Let the scenario conclusion appear.

Say:

> The model recomputes affected banks, recovery demand, capacity gaps, stranded reserve and system-level recovery.

Pause on the controlled-test callout.

> Individual Reserves and SCFR receive the same aggregate reserve pool.

## 1:35–1:58 — Robustness Sweep

Open **Model & Evidence** and scroll to **Robustness Sweep**.

Say:

> I also rerun a local grid around the current assumptions, so the project does not rely on one hand-picked baseline.

Point to:
- Positive Uplift;
- Max Uplift;
- matrix.

> Each cell is SCFR resilience minus Individual Reserve resilience under the same reserve budget.

## 1:58–2:25 — Evidence boundary + users

Show the top of Model & Evidence.

Say:

> The concentration-risk motivation is documented by BIS, EBA and DORA-related sources. The network and every numerical result here are synthetic.

Point to audience strip.

> The concept is designed for discussion by bank resilience teams, supervisors and financial-stability researchers.

## Final close — Controlled Comparison

Remain on **Controlled Comparison**.

Narration:

> Same shock. Same reserve budget. Different coordination. A plan B for one bank may not be a plan B for the system. Thank you for watching. My goal is to keep developing this research toward practical systemic cloud resilience in banking.

During “Thank you for watching,” a compact closing note fades in:

**THANK YOU · RESEARCH → PRACTICAL RESILIENCE**

Do not cut to a separate end card; keep the working product visible until the final frame.
